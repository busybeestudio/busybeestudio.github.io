How to Track Sales and Inventory at a Craft Fair (Step by Step)

Tracking a craft fair takes four habits: count what you bring, record every sale the same way, keep cash and card apart, and count again at the end of the day. Here is a simple way to do each one from your phone or on paper, with a worked example and what the numbers tell you for your next fair.

Step 1: Count what you bring

Before you pack, write a short list of every item you will sell: its name, its price and how many you are bringing. This is your opening count. Group small items that share a price (for example "stickers, $3") so the list stays short enough to use at a busy table.

Write the count down before you leave home, not at the booth. Setting up is rushed, and a count made while unpacking is easy to get wrong.

Step 2: Record every sale the same way

For each sale, note four things: what sold, how many, the price you charged, and whether it was cash or card. If you have a moment, the time of day is useful too.

On paper, the quickest method is a tally: one line per item on your list and a stroke for each one sold, crossed through every fifth stroke so you can count fast. On your phone, use one note or app for the whole day and add the sale before you hand over the bag, while you still remember the price. If you give a discount or a bundle deal, write the price actually paid, not the tag price.

Step 3: Keep cash and card apart

Start the day with a set amount of change in your cash box (your float) and write that number down. Mark each sale as cash or card as you go. Your card reader's app keeps its own list of card sales, so at the end of the day you only need to check that its total matches what you wrote down.

Step 4: Count again at the end of the day

When the fair closes, count what is left of each item before you pack it away. Then compare three numbers for each item:

  • Should be left = opening count minus the number you recorded as sold.
  • Actually left = what you just counted.
  • Difference = a sale you forgot to record, an item given away or damaged, or a miscount.

Do the same for cash: your float plus your cash sales should match what is in the box.

A worked example

Here is one item from an example day, with made-up numbers to show the method:

Brought (opening count)24
Recorded as sold17
Should be left (24 minus 17)7
Actually left at closing6
Difference to check1
Sell-through (17 sold of 24 brought)71%

The one missing candle is most likely a sale that was not written down during a rush. If it happens often, record sales before bagging, or ask a helper to keep the tally.

What the numbers tell you for the next fair

  • Best sellers: items with high sell-through deserve more stock and a front spot on the table next time.
  • Slow movers: items that barely sold may need a new price, a better display, or a bundle, or you can leave them at home.
  • Busy hours: if you noted the time, you know when to have a helper and when to restock the table.
  • Average sale: your total sales divided by the number of sales. If it is low, a simple bundle or a few higher-priced pieces can lift it.
  • Was the fair worth it: take your total sales and subtract the booth fee and your other costs for the day. Compare fairs by that number, not by how busy they felt.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an app to track craft fair sales?

No. A paper list with a tally works well, especially for a small table. A phone helps when you have many items or want totals worked out for you, but the four steps are the same either way.

What if my phone dies during the fair?

Keep a printed copy of your item list and a pen in your cash box. Switch to a paper tally until your phone is charged, then add those sales in.

How do I track card sales?

Your card reader's app lists every card payment. Still mark each sale as card on your own record, so you know what sold and can check that the totals match at the end of the day.

How often should I count my stock?

At least twice: before you leave home and after the fair closes. For a two-day fair, count at the end of each day so you know what to restock overnight.
